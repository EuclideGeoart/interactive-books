## Intuition

A point cloud can spread differently in different directions. Separate x and y variances describe the margins; covariance records whether centered coordinate changes tend to share a sign. An ellipse summarizes second moments without replacing the observed points.

## Definition

Weights $w_i\geq0$ have positive total W and normalized weights $p_i=w_i/W$. For column-vector observations $z_i=(x_i,y_i)^{\mathsf T}$, define $\mu=\sum_i p_i z_i$ and $\Sigma=\sum_i p_i(z_i-\mu)(z_i-\mu)^{\mathsf T}$. This is descriptive population covariance of the weighted empirical distribution, not an automatically unbiased sample-covariance formula.

## Mechanics

Select and move observed pairs through pointer, keyboard or numeric inputs. Edit a nonnegative integer weight; zero excludes that point from the statistical sum. The inspector exposes both marginal variances, their shared off-diagonal covariance, determinant and eigenvalues. The ellipse radii are square roots of eigenvalues, with a center at the weighted mean.

## Derivation

For any vector u, $u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u=\sum_i p_i[u^{\mathsf T}(z_i-\mu)]^2\geq0$. Thus Σ is symmetric positive semidefinite. In two dimensions this entails nonnegative marginal variances and determinant. Its orthonormal eigenvectors identify uncorrelated principal coordinates; eigenvalues measure spread along them.

## Worked examples

The canonical equally weighted pairs have mean (0,1). Their covariance is $\begin{bmatrix}4&14/5\\14/5&2\end{bmatrix}$ with determinant $4/25$. For diagonal covariance $\operatorname{diag}(4,1)$, the coordinate axes are principal directions and ellipse radii are 2 and 1. For equal diagonal entries and zero covariance, the shape is a circle with no unique orientation.

## Explore

Try a singular line, zero spread and equal eigenvalues. Change only the selected point’s weight and explain why the weighted center can move. Create negative covariance by moving pairs in opposite coordinate directions. Keep the actual observations in view while interpreting the ellipse.

## Common mistakes and counterexamples

A covariance ellipse is not automatically a 95% confidence region or a prediction region. A one-standard-deviation contour has no universal coverage fraction for an arbitrary point cloud. Singular covariance is valid when spread collapses to a line. Equal eigenvalues do not imply an invalid matrix; they mean orientation is not uniquely determined.

## Numerical considerations

All weighted means, covariance entries, determinant and rank classifications use exact fractions. The accepted 2×2 spectral model computes binary64 companions and checks eigenvector residuals. Exact determinant decides singularity; a drawing tolerance never changes mathematical rank. Coordinates lie in [−8,8] with at most two decimals; up to 12 observed pairs have weights 0–9 with positive total. The all-zero-weight state is rejected with an explanation.

## Engineering and ML connections

PCA rotates centered data toward directions of large variance. The first principal direction is useful as a preview, but interpretation still depends on feature units and scaling. Covariance of meters and milliseconds changes if units change; a large principal variance does not by itself identify a meaningful or causal feature.
