## Intuition

Scores can be negative or larger than one because they are not probabilities. Exponentiating makes relative scores positive; dividing by their total makes the outputs sum to one. A common offset changes the exponential scale but cancels out of the mathematical probabilities.

## Definition

For a nonempty finite logit vector $\mathbf z\in\mathbb R^n$, $p_i=\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf z)_i=e^{z_i}/\sum_j e^{z_j}$. Every mathematical output is positive and their sum is one. An optional explicit −∞ score denotes an excluded class with zero mass; at least one score must remain finite.

## Mechanics

Each score control changes one entry in the authoritative vector. The table exposes actual naive exponentials and probabilities alongside stable output. The inspector exposes the naive denominator, shifted denominator, log-sum-exp and selected class probability. Common finite shift changes the effective input vector, with a displayed probability difference relative to the original scores.

## Derivation

Sum the output probabilities to obtain $\sum_i e^{z_i}/\sum_j e^{z_j}=1$. For a common finite shift c, $e^{z_i+c}=e^ce^{z_i}$, so the common factor cancels: $\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf z+c\mathbf1)=\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf z)$. The same cancellation permits subtraction of the largest score before exponentiation.

## Worked examples

For logits $(\log2,0)$, exponentials are (2,1), denominator 3 and probabilities (2/3,1/3). For equal logits (0,0,0), outputs are (1/3,1/3,1/3). The canonical scores (2,1,0) produce approximately (0.665241,0.244728,0.090031). Adding 1000 preserves the mathematical ratios even though direct exponentials overflow.

## Common mistakes and counterexamples

Logits need not sum to one, and negative logits are not negative probabilities. Softmax is invariant to a common additive shift, not to arbitrary changes of relative score or multiplication by a common factor. The selected class is a user-chosen index; it is not secretly replaced by the argmax.

## Numerical considerations

The bounded explorer has 1–6 model classes and three canonical editable classes. Finite controls accept two-place scores and shifts in [−1000,1000]. Real exponentials are finite for finite scores, but binary64 exp(1000) overflows. Stable shifted exponentials are at most one. Normalization is checked at 32×epsilon×6, about 4.27e−14; independent Decimal400 reference probabilities have a separate 2e−14 absolute comparison tolerance. Adding a huge common shift in binary64 can round away the original score differences, so invariance is a mathematical identity with a numerical input-rounding caveat.

## Engineering and ML connections

Classification and vocabulary prediction use relative scores to define categorical probabilities. A logit is an input to this transformation, not an event probability. Cross entropy scores the resulting prediction against a target, and the next chapter retains log probabilities without taking a logarithm of underflowed outputs.
