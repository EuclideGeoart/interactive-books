## Intuition

Predicting an outcome poorly is costly when that outcome is common under the target. Cross entropy combines the target frequencies with the surprise assigned by a prediction. One observed label instead incurs just its own negative log probability.

## Definition

For normalized discrete distributions P and Q on the same ordered support, $H(P,Q)=-\sum_i p_i\log q_i$. P is the target; Q is the prediction. A realized label y has negative log-likelihood $L_y=-\log q_y$. For a one-hot target $p_i=\mathbf1\{i=y\}$, cross entropy equals that realized NLL.

## Mechanics

P starts fixed at (1/2,1/4,1/4). Only Q’s integer weights are editable and are normalized explicitly. The Target type selector deliberately replaces P by a one-hot observed class. The inspector keeps average cross entropy and the selected class’s NLL separate; the table and bars expose each actual contribution.

## Derivation

Take the expectation under P of the prediction’s negative log score: $\mathbb E_P[-\log q_X]=\sum_i p_i(-\log q_i)$. In the one-hot case, every term except i=y has zero target weight, leaving $-\log q_y$. An iid labeled dataset uses a sum of realized NLLs, or a mean whose denominator counts its contributing observations. Empirical class frequencies recover cross entropy if the same Q is used for all observations.

## Worked examples

For P=(1/2,1/4,1/4) and Q=(1/4,1/2,1/4), the bit contributions are (1,1/4,1/2), giving $H(P,Q)=7/4$ bits. If class 1 is the actual observed target, its NLL is 2 bits, which differs from that distribution average. For a target class with q=0, the NLL and one-hot cross entropy are infinite.

## Common mistakes and counterexamples

H(P,Q) generally differs from H(Q,P). The target supplies weights, not logarithm arguments. A zero target mass contributes zero even when prediction is zero; never evaluate 0×log 0. A positive target mass at zero prediction contributes +∞. Epsilon smoothing is a changed model, not a free mathematical repair.

## Numerical considerations

Both distributions are validated as exact rational masses with total exactly one. Predictions originate from visible nonnegative weights, whose all-zero vector is rejected. Logarithms are approximate; infinity arises from support mismatch rather than overflow. The finite sum is checked against Decimal100 at the declared 2e−12 scaled absolute/relative budget. The later logit pipeline computes NLL from stable log probabilities so probability underflow need not turn a finite NLL into infinity.

## Engineering and ML connections

Classification trains predicted class probabilities against labels. Language models use a vocabulary prediction at each position and the observed next token as the label. Distribution-averaged cross entropy describes expected log loss; the training batch is a finite average over its actual observed targets. That scalar feeds gradient-based optimization.
