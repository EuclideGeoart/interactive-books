## Intuition

The data likelihood describes how candidate parameters explain observations. A prior density weights candidates before these observations. Their product gives an unnormalized posterior; a separate normalizing integral gives the posterior distribution. MAP chooses a posterior density mode, while MLE chooses a likelihood maximum.

## Definition

A proper Beta prior has shapes $\alpha,\beta>0$ and density $p(\theta)=\theta^{\alpha-1}(1-\theta)^{\beta-1}/B(\alpha,\beta)$ on (0,1). Observed k successes and n−k failures give posterior $\operatorname{Beta}(a,b)$ with $a=\alpha+k$ and $b=\beta+n-k$. Evidence is $P(D)=B(a,b)/B(\alpha,\beta)$ for the fixed ordered sequence.

## Mechanics

Edit the actual sequence, candidate θ and prior shapes. The stage displays probability over observations, likelihood over candidates, prior, unnormalized posterior and normalized posterior. The inspector separates posterior mode, posterior mean, MLE and normalization factor. A prior changes the Bayesian result while leaving the data likelihood itself unchanged.

## Derivation

Adding exponents gives $p(\theta)L(\theta)\propto\theta^{a-1}(1-\theta)^{b-1}$. The Beta integral normalizes this expression. Only when a>1 and b>1 is the unique mode $(a-1)/(a+b-2)$ interior. Differentiating the log density proves this formula under those conditions; other shapes require their own boundary analysis.

## Worked examples

Prior Beta(2,2) and data [1,0,1,1,0] give Beta(5,4). MAP is 4/7, MLE is 3/5, and posterior mean is 5/9. Since $B(5,4)=1/280$ and $B(2,2)=1/6$, evidence is 3/140. At θ=1/2 the normalized posterior density is 35/16, which can exceed 1 because it is a density, not point probability.

## Explore

Use a uniform prior, an informative prior and the empty-data presets. Under a uniform prior and mixed nonempty data, MAP equals MLE; empty data leave the posterior equal to the prior. Inspect all-success data with uniform prior to see a boundary MAP. The two-boundary preset exposes divergent limits at both endpoints.

## Common mistakes and counterexamples

For a=b=1 the posterior is uniform and every parameter is a mode. If a≤1 and b≥1 with unequal shapes, the limiting mode is at 0; the symmetric case is at 1. If both shapes are below 1, both endpoint density limits diverge and no unique finite maximum is attained on (0,1). We explicitly use a limiting boundary-mode convention. A divergent density does not assign positive probability to a single endpoint. MAP is also sensitive to parameterization because density transforms with a Jacobian; it is not a coordinate-free summary.

## Numerical considerations

Prior shapes are restricted to half-integers from 0.5 to 5; observations are bounded at 32. This admits all taught mode cases while allowing log Gamma evaluation by Γ(1)=1, Γ(1/2)=√π and recurrence. Python independently uses math.lgamma; relative/absolute tolerance is 2e−12. Products combine exponents before endpoint evaluation, so a zero likelihood times a divergent prior never produces NaN. A finite plotting grid omits divergent endpoint values and reports their limits explicitly; it does not renormalize the sampled curve.

## Engineering and ML connections

Maximizing the log posterior is equivalent to minimizing negative log-likelihood minus log prior, up to a parameter-independent constant. This gives a statistical interpretation of some regularization penalties. The next optimization material concerns how an objective is searched; it does not supply or justify the prior. MAP, MLE and posterior mean remain distinct inferential choices.
