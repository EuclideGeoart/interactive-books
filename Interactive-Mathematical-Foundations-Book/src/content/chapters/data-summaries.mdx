## Intuition

The mean is a balance point: every observation contributes to the sum. The median asks where the ordered middle lies. A mode asks which observed value occurs most often. They answer different questions, so disagreement can be useful information. Try moving the selected 7 to 20 before reading on.

## Definition

For a nonempty dataset $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, the mean is $\bar x=n^{-1}\sum_i x_i$. Order the observations as $x_{(1)}\leq\cdots\leq x_{(n)}$. For odd $n$, the median is $x_{((n+1)/2)}$; for even $n$, average $x_{(n/2)}$ and $x_{(n/2+1)}$. Modes maximize observed frequency.

## Derivation

The mean uniquely minimizes $\sum_i(x_i-a)^2$: expanding around $\bar x$ gives $\sum_i(x_i-a)^2=\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2+n(a-\bar x)^2$. By contrast, a median minimizes the sum of absolute distances; for an even dataset a whole interval can minimize that objective, while our reported convention averages the middle pair.

## Worked examples

For $[1,2,2,3,7]$, the sum is 15, so the mean is 3. The middle observation is 2 and its frequency is 2: median and single mode are both 2. Replacing 7 by 20 gives mean $28/5$ and the same median 2. For $[1,1,3,3]$, both 1 and 3 are modes.

## Common mistakes and counterexamples

Do not choose a tied mode arbitrarily. When every distinct value occurs once, every value ties for maximal frequency; this explorer reports no repeated value and no uniquely determined mode. Some textbooks call every tied value a mode. This declared convention avoids implying a preferred value. A singleton has its own value as all three summaries.

## Numerical considerations

Inputs contain at most two decimal places and lie between −20 and 20; datasets have 1–24 observations. Sorting, sums, medians and frequency equality use exact rational arithmetic. Rendering converts these exact values to binary64 coordinates. Rounded inspector companions use ≈, while observed fractions use =. The plot reports data values, not unknown population parameters.

## Engineering and ML connections

Latency, sensor readings and feature distributions often have a long right tail. A mean can reveal total load, while a median describes a typical ordered observation. A mode is useful for discrete categories but depends on bin choices for continuous data; this chapter uses actual repeated values rather than inventing histogram modes.
