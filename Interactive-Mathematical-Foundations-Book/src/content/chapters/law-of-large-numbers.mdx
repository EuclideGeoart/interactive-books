## Intuition: a sum grows, but each new contribution gets diluted

A new observation enters the mean with weight $1/m$ at step $m$. Early observations can move it dramatically; later ones usually have less influence. Random fluctuations continue, but averaging independent information can make their collective effect smaller.

This is a statement about averages. Individual observations do not converge to the population mean: a fair Bernoulli draw remains either $0$ or $1$ even after a million earlier draws. The running mean is a distinct variable built from the complete prefix.

## Definition

For independent identically distributed observations $X_1,X_2,\ldots$ with $\mathbb E[|X_1|]<\infty$, write $\mu=\mathbb E[X_1]$ and

$$
\bar X_m=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^mX_j.
$$

The **strong Law of Large Numbers** states that $\bar X_m\to\mu$ almost surely. In words, convergence holds on an event of probability one in the ideal infinite iid experiment. It does not require each finite error to decrease or give a deterministic sample size that makes every possible path accurate.

A weaker convergence-in-probability statement is enough for many applications: for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, $\mathbb P(|\bar X_m-\mu|>\varepsilon)\to0$. The finite-variance argument below shows this weak statement under a stronger moment assumption.

## Mechanics: actual observations, explicit seed

The explorer's Bernoulli source has success probability $p$, entered as a percent. Its mean and variance are $p$ and $p(1-p)$ when $p$ is expressed as a probability. Uniform draws on $[0,1)$ have mean $1/2$ and variance $1/12$. Exponential rate-$1$ draws have mean and variance $1$ and are visibly skewed rather than Gaussian.

The canonical run uses Bernoulli probability $1/4$ (25 percent), seed $42$, and $200$ trials. Set a uint32 seed and a trial count from $1$ to $4000$. Replay regenerates the same observations; every prefix mean comes from those observations. Reset restores the canonical source, seed, and count. The short and large presets use $10$ and $2000$ trials; the maximum uses $4000$. The trace inspects finite prefixes rather than replacing them with idealized trajectories.

The explicit pseudo-random generator updates integer state by $(1664525s+1013904223)\bmod 2^{32}$ and divides by $2^{32}$. Bernoulli draws compare the result with $p$; uniform draws use it directly; exponential draws apply $-\log(1-u)$. A deterministic pseudo-random stream is a simulation device, not a mathematical proof of independence or a source of cryptographic randomness.

## Derivation: shrinking variance of the mean

For finite variance $\sigma^2$ and independent observations, the covariance terms vanish:

$$
\mathbb E[\bar X_m]=\mu,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(\bar X_m)
=\frac1{m^2}\sum_{j=1}^m\operatorname{Var}(X_j)
=\frac{\sigma^2}{m}.
$$

Thus the standard deviation of a repeated-sample mean is $\sigma/\sqrt m$. Chebyshev's inequality gives

$$
\mathbb P(|\bar X_m-\mu|\geq\varepsilon)
\leq\frac{\sigma^2}{m\varepsilon^2}.
$$

This bound tends to zero for fixed positive $\varepsilon$, establishing convergence in probability in this finite-variance case. It is a probability bound over repetitions; it does not prohibit a particular run from exceeding a chosen error or prove the stronger almost-sure statement by itself.

## Common mistakes and counterexamples

The law does not promise monotone improvement, exact equality at a finite step, or predictable compensation after a streak. A fair coin that has produced many successes still has success probability $1/2$ on its next independent trial. The average can adjust without the coin remembering past imbalance.

If all observations repeat one common random variable $Y$, then $\bar X_m=Y$ at every $m$: there is no variance reduction. Common marginals alone do not supply independence. An iid Cauchy source gives a different warning: it lacks a finite absolute first moment and has no finite population mean to which this LLN version can assert convergence.

## Numerical considerations

The simulation is bounded and uses the exact integer seed transition. Uniform $u$ lies in $[0,1)$, so the exponential transform never asks for $\log(0)$. The code uses `log1p` to evaluate $\log(1-u)$ accurately near zero. Floating observations and running means are numerical approximations, even when the model's moments have exact rational forms.

Finite simulation length, pseudo-random generator quality, floating accumulation, and sampling variability are separate limitations. A plotted $\sigma/\sqrt m$ scale describes theoretical spread under iid assumptions. It is neither a confidence interval nor a guarantee that the observed error lies inside it; interpreting coverage requires an additional probability statement.

## Engineering and ML connections

A Monte Carlo estimate averages model-generated quantities. Its target is an expectation, and independent finite-variance samples give a standard-deviation scale proportional to $1/\sqrt m$. Reaching half the scale typically requires four times the work; reducing variance can be as valuable as drawing more samples.

Training batches, simulation draws, and measured data can be dependent. Before using iid averaging intuition, inspect how examples are sampled and whether the intended expectation exists. More correlated observations need not provide the same information as more independent ones.
