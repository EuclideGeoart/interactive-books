## Intuition

A score offset can make every direct exponential enormous or tiny while leaving relative probabilities unchanged. Removing that common scale protects normalization. A very small final probability can still underflow, but its logarithm can be computed without constructing that tiny number.

## Definition

For a nonempty score vector with at least one finite entry, let $m=\max_i z_i$. Then $\operatorname{LSE}(\mathbf z)=\log\sum_i e^{z_i}=m+\log\sum_i e^{z_i-m}$ and $\log p_i=z_i-\operatorname{LSE}(\mathbf z)$. Compute the latter equivalently as $(z_i-m)-\log\sum_j e^{z_j-m}$ to keep the correction after centering.

## Derivation

Factor out $e^m$ from the exponential sum. Taking log yields $m+\log\sum_i e^{z_i-m}$. Every finite shifted score is at most zero, at least one is zero, and the shifted denominator lies in [1,n]. Thus unnecessary exponential overflow disappears. For log-softmax, algebraically subtract the correction after centering; this avoids subtracting two huge rounded values. Computing $\log$ of a final rounded probability would discard information already present in the scores.

## Worked examples

At (1000,999,998), m=1000, shifted scores (0,−1,−2), shifted exponentials $(1,e^{-1},e^{-2})$, denominator approximately 1.503215 and LSE approximately 1000.407606. At (1000,0,−1000), final binary64 probabilities are (1,0,0), but stable log probabilities remain (0,−1000,−2000). At two equal scores near $10^{308}$, the rounded LSE equals the common score; the centered log probabilities still equal $-\log2$.

## Common mistakes and counterexamples

Stabilization cannot guarantee that every tiny output probability is representable. Numerical probability zero from finite scores does not mean impossible support. A mathematical −∞ input explicitly excludes a class. All −∞ has a zero denominator and no unique distribution; inventing uniform probabilities would silently change the model. Positive infinity and NaN are rejected here rather than assigned an arbitrary limiting rule.

## Numerical considerations

Empty vectors, more than six entries, NaN and +∞ are invalid. An isolated −∞ is accepted as excluded support; all −∞ is undefined. Finite max-shift subtraction can itself exceed binary64 range at extreme opposite scores; that −∞ log result is labeled numerical range loss, not mathematical impossibility. Stable output comes from the evaluated max-shift algorithm. Direct exponential normalization with 400-digit Decimal arithmetic provides an independent comparison for feasible scores; analytic equal/dominant identities provide references for enormous-score examples. Logs use a 2e−12 scaled absolute/relative comparison tolerance; probabilities use 2e−14 absolute error. Normalization budget is 32×epsilon×6. These are numerical allowances, not proofs of analytic identities.
