## Intuition

A long chain of likely events can have a tiny joint probability. The multiplication path can lose that positive value to underflow. Adding logs retains a useful finite score even when converting the score back to a probability underflows.

## Definition

For independent observed events with probabilities $p_i\in[0,1]$, $L=\prod_i p_i$. For strictly positive factors, the natural log-likelihood is $\ell=\log L=\sum_i\log p_i$. Here a repeated block gives $L=(\prod_i p_i)^r$. Independence is an explicit modeling assumption; correlated events require conditional factors.

## Mechanics

The three factor controls and repeated-block count own the state. The table shows exact rational factors and approximate log factors. The graph plots log-likelihood after each sampled block; the inspector shows the same final quantity alongside the actual direct multiplication result.

## Derivation

The identity $\log(ab)=\log a+\log b$ follows because if $a=e^u$ and $b=e^v$, then $ab=e^{u+v}$. Repeating it yields $\ell$. Logs are increasing, so maximizing positive likelihood and maximizing its log choose the same parameters. Changing to negative log-likelihood turns that maximization into minimization.

## Worked examples

At $p=(1/2,1/4,4/5)$, $L=1/10$ and $\ell=-\log 10\approx-2.302585$ nats. At three factors of $1/2$ repeated 400 times, $L=2^{-1200}>0$ and $\ell=-1200\log2\approx-831.776617$. Binary64 cannot represent that positive product, but the log remains finite.

## Common mistakes and counterexamples

Log probability is nonpositive; surprise is its negative. A finite log does not make an impossible event possible. Substituting epsilon for zero changes the model. Multiplying marginal probabilities without independence is not generally a joint probability.

## Numerical considerations

Factors use exact decimal probabilities with at most two places; there are 1–6 supported factors and 1–1200 repetitions. Exact products remain BigInt fractions. Long exact products are displayed as an equivalent power of the exact block product. The approximate log is evaluated from the factors, independently checked against Decimal100 log of the exact product. Direct multiplication and exponentiating the log can underflow. The extended log of zero is −∞; negative, non-finite or out-of-range probability inputs are rejected.
