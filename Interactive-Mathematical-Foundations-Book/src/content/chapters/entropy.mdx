## Intuition

A point mass always produces the same outcome and has no uncertainty. Spreading fixed total mass over several outcomes creates uncertainty. Entropy asks how surprising an observation is on average under that distribution.

## Definition

For a finite discrete distribution $P=(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$ with $p_i\ge0$ and $\sum_i p_i=1$, $H(P)=-\sum_i p_i\log p_i=\mathbb E_P[I(X)]$. Natural logs give nats; base-2 logs give bits. Define the zero-mass contribution by $0\log0=0$.

## Mechanics

The controls are nonnegative integer weights. The displayed rule pᵢ=wᵢ/Σwⱼ normalizes those weights once into authoritative exact masses. The probability graph and contribution bars both use that derived distribution; bar lengths encode actual pᵢIᵢ values, which also appear in the table. An all-zero weight vector is rejected.

## Derivation

Take the expectation of surprise: each $-\log p_i$ occurs with probability $p_i$. The limit $\lim_{p\downarrow0}p\log p=0$ follows by writing $p=e^{-t}$, so $p\log p=-te^{-t}\to0$. Every positive-mass surprise is nonnegative, so $H\ge0$. Equality requires all positive mass to be at probability 1. For the upper bound, strict concavity of log gives $\sum_{p_i>0}p_i\log(1/p_i)\le\log(\sum_{p_i>0}1)\le\log n$. Equality at fixed support n requires every $p_i=1/n$.

## Worked examples

For $P=(1/2,1/4,1/4)$, surprises are $(1,2,2)$ bits and contributions are $(1/2,1/2,1/2)$ bits. Thus $H(P)=3/2$ bits. For a uniform three-outcome distribution, $H=\log_2 3\approx1.584963$ bits. For $(1,0,0)$, every contribution is zero.

## Explore

Compare Point mass and Uniform masses without changing support size. Then move weight into one outcome and identify the contribution that changes. Make one weight zero and explain why its infinite single-event surprise contributes zero to the expectation.

## Common mistakes and counterexamples

Entropy belongs to a distribution; surprise belongs to one observation. A rare event can have huge surprise while contributing little to entropy. Entropy does not ignore probabilities and merely count labels. A zero-weight label still belongs to the fixed support used for the maximum comparison.

## Numerical considerations

Supported distributions have 1–6 outcomes with integer weights up to 1,000,000 in the model (controls use 0–100). Exact rational normalization preserves total mass exactly. Zero terms are branched before evaluating products; no 0×∞ is computed. Logarithms and entropy sums use binary64 and are checked against exact Fraction/Decimal100 sources at 2e−12 scaled absolute/relative budget. Numerical examples verify implementation; concavity supplies the mathematical maximum proof.

## Engineering and ML connections

Entropy is the best distribution-averaged log loss attainable by predicting the target distribution itself. Cross entropy will measure a prediction distribution against this baseline, and KL will expose the excess loss.
