## Intuition

At a fixed model parameter, probability tells us how plausible different observations are. After a sequence is observed, likelihood asks how plausible that same fixed sequence is under different candidate parameters. The formula is shared; the variable being explored changes.

## Definition

For observed binary data $D=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$, with k successes, the iid Bernoulli model assigns $P_\theta(D)=\prod_i\theta^{x_i}(1-\theta)^{1-x_i}$. Viewed as a function of $\theta\in[0,1]$, this is $L(\theta;D)=\theta^k(1-\theta)^{n-k}$. The natural log-likelihood is $\ell(\theta)=k\log\theta+(n-k)\log(1-\theta)$ wherever the factors are positive.

## Mechanics

The numbered buttons display actual observations; activating one changes that datum explicitly. Adding successes or failures changes the dataset. Moving candidate θ only reevaluates the fixed data: it never resamples them. Probability bars show the two possible next observations at that θ; the curve instead plots candidate parameters on its horizontal axis.

## Derivation

Independence multiplies one probability factor for each observed datum. Collect the k success factors and n−k failure factors to obtain the power form. Taking logs turns a product into a sum. An empty product equals 1 and its log equals 0; a factor appearing zero times contributes no logarithm, even at θ=0 or θ=1.

## Worked examples

For fixed data [1,0,1,1,0] and $\theta=3/5$, $L=(3/5)^3(2/5)^2=108/3125$. For one success, $L(\theta)=\theta$ and $\int_0^1L(\theta)\,d\theta=1/2$, showing that likelihood need not normalize over the parameter. For an ordered sequence [1,0,1] at θ=1/2, likelihood is 1/8; the count event of exactly two successes has probability 3/8.

## Common mistakes and counterexamples

Likelihood is not generally a posterior density and need not integrate to one over θ. Model probabilities normalize over possible data at fixed θ. A binomial coefficient appears for an observed success-count event, but not for one fixed ordered sequence. Impossible observations cannot be deleted to rescue a candidate.

## Numerical considerations

Observed data have length 0–32 and contain only 0 or 1. Candidate probabilities have two decimal places and lie in [0,1]. Products are exact fractions; natural logs use binary64 log and log1p with absent factors skipped. A zero likelihood has extended log −∞, which is displayed explicitly rather than sent into a finite numeric renderer. This bounded explorer does not underflow its supported positive products; larger applications still need log-space arithmetic.

## Engineering and ML connections

Probabilistic classifiers assign model probabilities to labeled observations. Combining independent examples produces a likelihood; minimizing negative log-likelihood turns their joint assessment into an additive objective. Dependence or model misspecification changes what that objective means. The later optimization course examines how to search parameter space.
