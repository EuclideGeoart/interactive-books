## Intuition: split a vector into reachable and unreachable parts

Projecting onto one direction separates a parallel component from a perpendicular residual. A subspace works the same way: find a vector inside the subspace whose leftover is perpendicular to **every** direction in that subspace.

For a system $Ax=b$, the reachable vectors form the column space of $A$. If $b$ lies outside it, no coefficient vector can solve every equation. Least squares chooses the closest reachable vector. The residual records precisely what the transformation cannot match.

## Definition

Vectors are orthogonal when their dot product is zero. An orthogonal basis consists of independent, pairwise perpendicular nonzero vectors. An **orthonormal** basis additionally gives each vector norm one. If $q_1,\ldots,q_r$ are orthonormal, the projection onto their span is

$$
p=\sum_{j=1}^r(q_j^Tb)q_j,\qquad r=b-p.
$$

Least squares minimizes $\|Ax-b\|^2$. The minimizer is unique when $A$ has full column rank. With deficient rank, all minimizers are $x_0+z$ for $z\in\ker A$, but their projected output $Ax_0$ is unique.

## Derivation: Gram–Schmidt exposes projection mechanics

Begin with $u_1=a_1$. Remove the components of each later column along earlier nonzero orthogonal directions:

$$
u_k=a_k-\sum_{j<k}\frac{a_k^Tu_j}{u_j^Tu_j}u_j.
$$

A zero residual means the column was dependent; an original zero vector also cannot be normalized. The lesson records those failures and continues with surviving independent directions. Dividing by $\|u_j\|$ then gives an orthonormal basis $q_j$. In the exact model we project using the unnormalized orthogonal directions, avoiding unnecessary irrational arithmetic:

$$
p=\sum_j\frac{b^Tu_j}{u_j^Tu_j}u_j.
$$

The exact dot products certify orthogonality; normalized unit-vector drawings are approximate companions. No zero direction is ever divided by its norm.

## Derivation: normal equations and optimality

Suppose $p=Ax_0$ and $r=b-p$ is perpendicular to the column space. For any coefficient change $h$, $Ah$ belongs to that space. Pythagoras gives

$$
\|A(x_0+h)-b\|^2=\|Ah-r\|^2=\|Ah\|^2+\|r\|^2.
$$

Therefore no change can improve the error. Equality occurs precisely when $Ah=0$, explaining nonunique minimizers through the null space.

The orthogonality condition is $A^T(b-Ax_0)=0$, equivalently

$$
A^T(Ax_0-b)=0,\qquad A^TAx_0=A^Tb.
$$

These are the normal equations. They are a mathematical characterization of a minimum, not a promise that every original equation has been solved.

## Common mistakes

A residual can be nonzero while being orthogonal to every column. Least squares minimizes the sum of **squared** residuals, not their signed sum. The segments in the fit are vertical prediction errors, not shortest distances to the drawn line. Nonunique coefficients do not imply multiple closest projected data vectors. Orthonormality requires both perpendicularity and unit length.

## Numerical considerations

This small educational domain uses exact rational Gram–Schmidt and exact projection, so zero and rank classifications do not depend on an epsilon. Normalized companion vectors and rendered segments use binary64. In numerical software, classical Gram–Schmidt can lose orthogonality; modified Gram–Schmidt or Householder QR are preferable depending on the problem.

The live sensitivity cue appears when the second column’s relative orthogonal remainder is below $0.05$; it describes nearly parallel columns and never changes exact rank.

Forming $A^TA$ squares the 2-norm condition number for full-column-rank $A$. That can amplify roundoff even though the normal equations are mathematically correct. QR and SVD methods usually provide a safer practical route. Numerical rank tolerances must reflect scale and uncertainty and can legitimately differ from the exact rank of entered rationals.

## Engineering and ML connections

Linear regression projects targets onto features. Redundant feature columns produce unidentifiable parameter directions but still define a closest predicted target vector. Orthogonal features simplify coefficient calculations. SVD extends this picture by organizing the input and output directions by strength and explaining a minimum-norm pseudoinverse solution.
