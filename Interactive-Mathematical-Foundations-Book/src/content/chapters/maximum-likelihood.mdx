## Intuition

A candidate parameter can agree more or less well with the fixed observations. MLE selects the candidate that assigns those observations the greatest likelihood. It is an estimator defined by a rule; its result changes when different data are observed.

## Definition

For a closed candidate domain $[0,1]$, define $\operatorname{argmax}_{\theta\in[0,1]}L(\theta;D)$. For nonempty Bernoulli data the unique maximizing estimate is $\hat\theta=k/n$, including 0 and 1 when failures or successes are complete. For empty data, every candidate ties because $L=1$.

## Mechanics

Move candidate θ and inspect its exact likelihood, natural log-likelihood and gap from the maximum. The dashed marker and inspector show the data-derived optimizer independently of the candidate control. The optimum may be an exact fraction that cannot be entered with the control’s two-decimal precision; its marker and reported value retain that exact mathematical estimate.

## Derivation

For $0<k<n$, $\ell\prime(\theta)=k/\theta-(n-k)/(1-\theta)=0$ gives $\theta=k/n$. The second derivative $\ell\prime\prime(\theta)=-k/\theta^2-(n-k)/(1-\theta)^2<0$ proves a unique interior maximum. For k=n, likelihood increases to 1; for k=0, it decreases from 0. These cases use boundary monotonicity rather than an invalid interior derivative equation.

## Worked examples

The canonical sequence has 3 successes out of 5, so MLE is 3/5. A candidate 2/5 gives likelihood 2/3 of the maximum, and the log-likelihood gap is $\log(3/2)\approx0.405465$. All successes give MLE 1; all failures give MLE 0. No observations give no unique optimizer, not a default estimate of 1/2.

## Common mistakes and counterexamples

Do not maximize over hypothetical data at a fixed parameter; that asks for a most probable outcome. MLE maximizes over parameters with the observed data fixed. MLE does not automatically provide a confidence interval, a posterior probability or a causal interpretation. Boundary estimates can be correct optimizers even when they are fragile predictions for new observations.

## Numerical considerations

The MLE k/n, exact candidate likelihood and maximum likelihood remain rational. Floating log-likelihood comparisons use natural logs and stable log1p. At a zero-likelihood candidate the gap is +∞; both non-finite values receive explicit messages. Derived maximizing fractions are never rounded back into the two-decimal input state. No general optimizer or expression evaluator is needed for this analytic model.

## Engineering and ML connections

Bernoulli MLE is the empirical success rate. More complex probabilistic models may require numerical optimization, but the statistical objective and the search algorithm remain distinct. Training likelihood assesses observed data; useful deployment predictions also require validation, uncertainty analysis and model checking.
