## Intuition: rows become columns

The transpose turns a matrix around its main diagonal. This simple exchange lets matrix multiplication collect column dot-products:

$$
(Q^{\mathsf T}Q)_{ij}=\mathbf q_i\cdot\mathbf q_j.
$$

The diagonal entries are squared column lengths. The off-diagonal entries are pairwise dot products. One product therefore asks whether the columns form an orthonormal frame.

## Definition

For an $m\times n$ matrix $A$, its transpose $A^{\mathsf T}$ is the $n\times m$ matrix defined by

$$
(A^{\mathsf T})_{ij}=A_{ji}.
$$

A square matrix $Q$ is **orthogonal** when

$$
Q^{\mathsf T}Q=I.
$$

Equivalently, its columns are unit vectors and mutually orthogonal. In two dimensions, these columns form a rigid coordinate frame.

## Mechanics: a certificate in one product

For $Q=[\mathbf q_1\ \mathbf q_2]$,

$$
Q^{\mathsf T}Q=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf q_1\cdot\mathbf q_1&\mathbf q_1\cdot\mathbf q_2\\
\mathbf q_2\cdot\mathbf q_1&\mathbf q_2\cdot\mathbf q_2
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The identity result says both columns have length one and their dot product is zero. A shear fails through a nonzero off-diagonal entry. Unequal scaling fails through a diagonal entry other than one.

## Derivation: dot products, lengths, and angles survive

If $Q^{\mathsf T}Q=I$, then for any $\mathbf x,\mathbf y$,

$$
(Q\mathbf x)\cdot(Q\mathbf y)
=(Q\mathbf x)^{\mathsf T}(Q\mathbf y)
=\mathbf x^{\mathsf T}Q^{\mathsf T}Q\mathbf y
=\mathbf x^{\mathsf T}\mathbf y.
$$

Setting $\mathbf y=\mathbf x$ proves $\|Q\mathbf x\|^2=\|\mathbf x\|^2$. Since dot products and lengths determine the cosine formula, angles between nonzero vectors are preserved too.

## The transpose as an inverse—only here

The equation $Q^{\mathsf T}Q=I$ says that $Q^{\mathsf T}$ undoes $Q$. For a square orthogonal matrix,

$$
Q^{-1}=Q^{\mathsf T}.
$$

This is a special structural shortcut, not a rule for arbitrary matrices. A shear’s transpose does not undo the shear, and a scaling transpose is usually the same scaling rather than its reciprocal. General inverses remain for II.16.

## Rotations and reflections

A planar rotation by angle $\theta$ has columns $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ and $(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$. A reflection also has orthonormal columns but reverses handedness. The interactive presets use exact quarter-turn and axis-reflection entries so the certificate is exact rather than tolerance-based.

## Worked examples

For

$$
Q=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
Q^{\mathsf T}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{bmatrix},
$$

direct multiplication gives $Q^{\mathsf T}Q=I$. The vector $(3,4)$ becomes $(-4,3)$; both have length $5$.

## Explore

Swap the two identity columns and identify the resulting reflection. Negate both columns and recognize a half-turn. Give one column length two and inspect the diagonal failure. Make unit-length columns that are not perpendicular, then perpendicular columns with unequal lengths. Explain each entry of $Q^{\mathsf T}Q$.

## Common mistakes

Transposition does not negate entries, and it does not reverse the order inside one vector. Perpendicular columns alone are not orthonormal unless each has unit length. Orthogonal matrices need not be symmetric, and reflections are not excluded. Most importantly, $A^{-1}=A^{\mathsf T}$ is false without the orthogonality certificate.

## Numerical considerations

Exact entered decimals give an exact $Q^{\mathsf T}Q$ in this lesson. Floating-point code typically tests whether it is close to $I$ with a scale-aware tolerance. That numerical test is evidence about stored approximations, not a redefinition of orthogonality. Angle calculations should also guard zero vectors and clamp only tiny endpoint roundoff before applying arccos.

## Engineering and ML connections

Orthogonal transforms preserve signal energy and avoid stretching some directions more than others. They appear in rigid motion, numerical factorization, initialization, and feature rotations. Their transpose is cheap to apply and stable as an inverse, but learned matrices are not automatically orthogonal unless a model or algorithm enforces that structure.
