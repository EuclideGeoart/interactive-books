## Intuition

Before sampling, an interval is random because its center depends on unseen data. The population mean stays fixed. After observing a sample, the computed interval has fixed endpoints: it either contains that mean or misses it. Repeating the procedure makes this distinction visible.

## Definition

Assume $X_i\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(\mu,\sigma^2)$ with known $\sigma>0$. For confidence $1-\alpha$, define $I(X)=[\bar X-z_{1-\alpha/2}\sigma/\sqrt n,\bar X+z_{1-\alpha/2}\sigma/\sqrt n]$. The normal quantile satisfies $\Phi(z_{1-\alpha/2})=1-\alpha/2$. Confidence is the procedure’s coverage $P_\mu(\mu\in I(X))=1-\alpha$.

## Mechanics

The true mean is fixed at 2. Change known σ, sample size, repetition count, confidence level or seed. Each interval comes from its own independently generated sample, whose observed values and endpoints are available in the table. Covered intervals use solid lines and circles; misses use dashed lines and crosses. Replay preserves the run; Reset restores the canonical experiment.

## Derivation

Normality gives $Z=(\bar X-\mu)/(\sigma/\sqrt n)\sim N(0,1)$ for every positive n. The event $-z\leq Z\leq z$ is equivalent to $\mu\in I(X)$ and has probability $1-\alpha$. This is an exact finite-sample normal-model statement; no large-sample approximation or estimated σ is hidden in the derivation.

## Worked examples

At 95% confidence, $z\approx1.959963984540054$. Known $\sigma=1.5$ and $n=8$ give half-width approximately 1.03943, so an observed mean of 2 gives endpoints approximately 0.960572 and 3.03943. If the mean instead equals 4, the interval approximately [2.96057,5.03943] misses the fixed true mean 2. The simulation calculates its actual means without replacing them by either example.

## Explore

## Common mistakes and counterexamples

A 95% frequentist interval does not assign 95% posterior probability to the fixed parameter after seeing the data. A posterior statement requires a prior and a Bayesian model. Nor does nominal confidence promise that exactly 95 of the next 100 intervals will cover. Estimated σ needs a different normal-theory procedure using Student’s t; dependent or nonnormal data can invalidate this model’s coverage.

## Numerical considerations

The 90%, 95% and 99% normal critical values are explicit binary64 constants, independently checked against Python NormalDist. Endpoints and Box–Muller normal samples are binary64 approximations; the reference-comparison tolerance is 2e−12 times max(1, absolute expected value). Analytic coverage uses the accepted normal CDF with absolute interval-probability error at most 2e−9. The seeded generator consumes two uniforms per observation and discards the sine partner; 1−u is positive so logarithms stay finite. The experiment stores at most 80 samples of 64 observations. Coverage classifications compare the computed endpoints directly; no sample is adjusted to achieve nominal coverage.

## Engineering and ML connections

Repeated measurement and model evaluation often report intervals. Their validity depends on the data-generating assumptions and procedure, including selection, dependence and uncertainty in σ. More data narrow a valid interval but do not cure a biased collection process. MAP later introduces posterior inference with a prior; it has a different interpretation.
