## Shapes and bounded API

`pca(state)` in TypeScript and `pca(request)` in canonical Python accept 1–128 observations. Each is a nonempty unique string ID and exactly two dense finite real coordinates. X and Xc are n×2 row batches; μ and each eigenvector are 2×1 mathematical columns. Public `axes` serializes a list of two direction columns, so a conventional matrix V is formed by taking those arrays as columns. Vk is 2×k, Z is n×k, reconstruction/residual batches are n×2.

Normalization is required: `sample` divides by n−1, `population` by n. k must be a finite integral numeric value from 0 to 2; `1.0` and NumPy integral numeric values have the same meaning as `1`, while bool/string/null/nonintegral/nonfinite values are rejected. Python accepts only real scalar families int/float/np.integer/np.floating (excluding bool); complex values are rejected even with zero imaginary part. Both coordinates and k are normalized once to binary64 before arithmetic or integral/range checks, and integer k conversion uses that same normalized value. Extended-precision NumPy inputs therefore follow their binary64 interpretation, not their pre-conversion integral part or bounds. No kernel or dataset randomness exists, and no seed is claimed for synthetic hand-authored clouds. The SVD oracle's local seeded random challenge rows are solely test inputs, not browser provenance.

All shapes, array entries (including holes), IDs, coordinates and configuration are validated before singleton/constant/k=0 outcomes. Empty batches, ragged or sparse rows, excess dimensions and duplicate/missing IDs are rejected. Numeric lab edits additionally constrain coordinates to [−5,5]. Pointer positions snap to a 0.1 lattice; arrow edits add 0.1 or 0.5 to the current coordinate, preserving its finer precision; numeric entries retain their binary64 values. Exact equivalence is promised on the common edit lattice, not by rounding arbitrary numeric entries onto it.

## Computation

Each feature mean preserves an exact constant value, otherwise accumulating `(x_j−first_j)/n` before adding `first_j`. Centered products are part of the numerical result and feed the product inspector. Covariance uses centered sums rather than raw second-moment subtraction.

For C=[[a,b],[b,c]], divide entries by their maximum magnitude. The leading eigenvalue uses `(A+C)/2+hypot((A−C)/2,B)` times that scale. Choose the longer of `[L−C,B]` / `[B,L−A]`, normalize it and give its first nonzero component positive sign. The second vector is the perpendicular `[-v1_y,v1_x]`. A diagonal matrix selects the larger coordinate axis; a repeated diagonal spectrum chooses the coordinate basis by convention. Signs and repeated-eigenspace bases are not mathematically unique.

The small eigenvalue comes from `sum((x_ci·v2)^2)/q`; it does not subtract two nearly equal large terms or cancel a covariance determinant. Last-bit ordering swaps the pair/vectors/scores together if that energy rounds above the computed leading eigenvalue. Near repetition has a warning at gap ≤64 eps × total variance, but this does not merge eigenvalues, classify rank or manufacture zero values. Exact computed equality is reported separately. A positive small eigenvalue stays positive unless arithmetic cannot represent it; squared-component or energy-normalization underflow to zero is rejected. An eigenvector whose norm becomes zero after extreme scaling is rejected as unresolved rather than divided by zero. Every explicitly computed nonfinite product/sum/centering/reconstruction/metric is rejected. Binary64 may still lose input variation, cancel in a dot product, or round very extreme ratios; the API is educational, not arbitrary precision.

Scores are dot products with those directions. k=0 gives empty scores and mean-only reconstruction. k=1 reconstructs along v1 and adds μ. k=2 explicitly applies VVᵀ=I by copying source coordinates, while exposing actual two-component scores; it avoids a floating rotate/unrotate and yields exactly zero full-space residual/SSE. This identity shortcut is mathematically exact for the full retained space, not tolerance-based residual zeroing.

Residual = source − reconstruction. SSE sums squared vector norms, MSE divides SSE by n, and per-entry MSE divides by 2n. In exact arithmetic SSE=q·sum(discarded eigenvalues); binary64 identities are approximate except explicit full-space identity. Positive total variance gives ratios λj/sumλ and their retained sum. Zero total variance gives null ratios/retained fraction with a reason, while defined constant-data reconstruction has zero error. One population observation gives zero covariance and zero-spread ratios; one sample observation has centered data/mean but undefined covariance/PCA/reconstruction outputs. The browser shows the reason instead of fake outputs.
