## Increase capacity without moving the held-out targets

Move the degree slider from one to two to five. The curve changes because the design acquires columns $x^2,x^3,\ldots$; the solve remains linear least squares in its coefficients. Circles are noisy training observations. Squares are five held-out observations never used to fit the coefficients. Diamonds show each prediction, including held-out predictions.

At degree five, six coefficients can interpolate six distinct training inputs. Training MSE becomes essentially zero, yet held-out error rises above the quadratic fit. Inspect both values before choosing the degree. Editing a training target recomputes the fit; it never rewrites the held-out target column.

## Define a feature map, not a nonlinear parameter solve

For degree $q$, use

$$
\phi(x)=[1,x,x^2,\ldots,x^q],\quad W=[b,w_1,\ldots,w_q]^\mathsf T,
\quad \hat y=\phi(x)W.
$$

The curve is nonlinear in $x$ when $q>1$, but linear in the learned coefficients. For $n$ observations, $X_{ij}=x_i^j$ has shape $n\times(q+1)$, $W$ is $(q+1)\times1$, and targets/predictions are $n\times1$. Column zero is the intercept. The sheet exposes each actual feature row, not just a smooth curve.

Holding $X$ fixed, the derivation is unchanged: $\nabla_W L=(2/n)X^\mathsf T(XW-y)$. We use the rank-aware SVD solve from III-10. No inverse normal equations or iterative nonlinear fitting is introduced by taking powers of the inputs.

## Work the hand-authored training and held-out split

Training inputs are $[-1,-0.6,-0.2,0.2,0.6,1]$ with targets $[1.1,0.46,0,0.16,0.26,1.2]$. They were deliberately authored near $x^2$, with visible deviations. Held-out inputs are $[-0.8,-0.4,0,0.4,0.8]$ with exact illustrative targets $[0.64,0.16,0,0.16,0.64]$. Neither set is measured or randomly generated; there is no seed.

At $x=-0.6$, a quadratic feature row is $[1,-0.6,0.36]$. The fitted quadratic coefficients are approximately $[0.001875,0.0042857143,1.1316964286]^\mathsf T$. At the held-out input $x=0$, the row is $[1,0,0]$, so the prediction is the intercept $0.001875$ while the held-out target is zero.

## Capacity and evaluation answer different questions

Adding columns expands the representable prediction space, so the mathematical least-squares training optimum cannot get worse when the previous design is contained in the next. In finite precision, numerical rank truncation can disturb strict monotonicity. Neither statement promises monotonically improving held-out error.

Training loss measures the fit to rows that chose the coefficients. Held-out MSE measures errors on untouched rows. Using this held-out set repeatedly to select degree makes it a validation set; it should not then be advertised as an untouched final test. A future test set belongs to a separate evaluation step.

## Mistakes and numerical limits

Do not call the coefficients nonlinear merely because powers appear in the features. Do not compare training MSE at one degree with held-out MSE at another and pretend they are the same measurement. Do not fit or scale features using evaluation targets.

Raw powers become badly conditioned for high degree or large input magnitudes. This sheet supports degrees one through five and training inputs in $[-1,1]$; its hand-authored example needs no fitted scaling step. A repeated input can reduce rank. The SVD cutoff $10^{-10}\sigma_{\max}$ is visible; near-cutoff directions may be discarded. Powers, products and outputs must remain finite, and failures do not return invented predictions. Scientific notation distinguishes tiny computed losses from an exact zero.
