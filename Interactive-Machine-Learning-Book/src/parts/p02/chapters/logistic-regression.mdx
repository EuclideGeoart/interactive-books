## A linear score becomes a Bernoulli probability

With augmented design rows $[1,x_i]$, use intercept-first column $W=[b,w]^\mathsf T$:

$$
z_i=b+wx_i,\quad p_i=\sigma(z_i)=\frac1{1+e^{-z_i}}.
$$

The logit $z$ ranges over real numbers while $p$ lies between zero and one. Odds are $p/(1-p)=e^z$, so $z$ is log odds. Logistic regression is linear in the logit, not in the probability. The name does not make binary labels continuous regression targets.

For a binary label $y$, the Bernoulli likelihood is $p^y(1-p)^{1-y}$. Its negative logarithm gives

$$
\ell(z,y)=-y\log p-(1-y)\log(1-p).
$$

Across independent illustrative rows we minimize the mean, $L=(1/n)\sum_i\ell(z_i,y_i)$. This is the training objective; accuracy and confusion counts evaluate thresholded decisions and need not improve on every update.

## Derive a stable loss and its gradient

Substitute the sigmoid into the likelihood:

$$
\ell(z,y)=\log(1+e^z)-yz
=\max(z,0)-yz+\log(1+e^{-|z|}).
$$

The last expression never exponentiates a positive large argument. `log1p` retains a tiny correction near zero. Sigmoid itself branches on the logit's sign to avoid exponent overflow. We compute BCE from logits, rather than taking $\log(0)$ after a probability rounds to zero or one.

Differentiating yields $\partial\ell/\partial z=\sigma(z)-y$. Apply the chain rule to the linear score:

$$
\nabla_WL=\frac1nX^\mathsf T(p-y),\qquad W^+=W-\eta\nabla_WL.
$$

Unlike full MSE, this gradient has no factor of two. The intercept entry averages $p-y$; the weight entry averages $x(p-y)$. All entries update simultaneously from the same old probabilities. The shape is $d\times1$, matching $W$.

## Extreme scores and decision counterexamples

For $z=1000,y=0$, stable BCE is approximately $1000$ even though sigmoid rounds to one. For $z=-1000,y=1$, it is again $1000$. For confidently correct labels it underflows toward a finite zero loss. Rounded probability endpoints do not make an impossible observation cost zero.

At threshold $0.5$, $z=0$ is positive because equality counts. The canonical predictions are $[0,0,1,1,1]$: two true positives, one false positive, two true negatives and no false negatives. A gradient can reduce BCE without flipping any label. Conversely a small threshold change can flip a label without changing BCE at all.

## Mistakes and numerical limits

Do not train on a thresholded label: the differentiable score carries information that a zero/one decision discards. Do not use the MSE derivative for a BCE objective. Scores from this toy model are estimates; no calibration claim or causal interpretation is made.

Inputs and arithmetic must be finite; labels must be exactly zero or one. The pure kernel permits 1–64 rows and 1–7 design columns. The sheet bounds coefficients to ±1000, editable features to −3…3 and rates to $0.001\ldots1$. Invalid edits preserve the current state. Large logits can saturate probabilities and gradients, while a mathematically infinite separation optimum need not yield finite learned coefficients. There is no claim of reaching a global fitted solution after three updates.
