## Define the unsupervised problem

We observe feature vectors but have no target labels. A cluster label is an algorithm's grouping decision. It is not an observed class, a predicted regression target, or an ordered numerical quantity.

For $n$ observations and chosen $k$, k-means uses centers $c_j$ and assignments $a_i$. This sheet measures squared scaled Euclidean distance:

$$
d_{ij}^2=\sum_{r=1}^2\left(\frac{x_{ir}-c_{jr}}{s_r}\right)^2,
\qquad s_r>0,\qquad
J(a,c)=\sum_{i=1}^n d_{i,a_i}^2.
$$

$J$ is the within-cluster sum of squares, WCSS. It is a **sum**, without $1/n$, one half, or a square root. Each observation has weight one, including coincident observations with different IDs. Reference scales remain fixed during a Lloyd run. With unit scales, this is ordinary Euclidean k-means. There is no intercept or target column.

Mathematical observations are $2\times1$ columns; $X$ stores samples in its $n\times2$ rows. The center matrix is $k\times2$, the squared-distance matrix $n\times k$, and assignments an $n\times1$ integer column. Zero-based cluster indices run from 0 to $k-1$.

## Two exact subproblems give the mechanics

With centers fixed, choose the least costly center independently for every row:

$$
a_i\leftarrow\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{0\le j<k}d_{ij}^2.
$$

The browser and NumPy choose the lowest center index on an exact computed tie. Rounding the table to six significant digits does not create a tie in the model.

With assignments fixed, let $C_j$ contain the observations assigned to center $j$. For a nonempty cluster, differentiate each coordinate of its quadratic cost:

$$
\frac{\partial J_j}{\partial c_{jr}}
=\frac{2}{s_r^2}\left(|C_j|c_{jr}-\sum_{i\in C_j}x_{ir}\right)=0,
\qquad
c_{jr}\leftarrow\frac{1}{|C_j|}\sum_{i\in C_j}x_{ir}.
$$

Thus the coordinatewise arithmetic mean minimizes that cluster's cost. Fixed feature scaling changes memberships but does not change the raw-coordinate mean of a fixed membership. The positive quadratic curvature gives the unique mean for a nonempty cluster.

The equivalent identity is useful for an independent proof:

$$
\sum_{i\in C_j}\left\|S^{-1}(x_i-c_j)\right\|^2
=\sum_{i\in C_j}\left\|S^{-1}(x_i-\mu_j)\right\|^2
+|C_j|\left\|S^{-1}(c_j-\mu_j)\right\|^2.
$$

The mean removes the last nonnegative term. Assignment and update cannot increase $J$ in exact arithmetic under these fixed data, scales and declared empty-cluster conventions. Arbitrary dragging changes centers and can increase it.

The objective is $0+4+0+4=8$. Cluster 0 contains s1/s2, sum (−4,0), count 2, mean (−2,0). Cluster 1 contains s3/s4, sum (4,0), count 2, mean (2,0). At those proposed centers each assigned squared distance is 1, so proposed WCSS is 4. Pressing **Update means** commits exactly those values.

The next assignment is unchanged. A second mean update also leaves the centers unchanged, so the sheet stops at binary64 equality. Its trace objectives are **8 → 8 → 4 → 4 → 4**, including the initial preview and each assignment/update phase. Reaching this fixed point does not generally prove global optimality.

## Explore without changing history

The centroid selector, numeric fields, pointer dragging and arrow keys all call the same coordinate edit. Pointer input snaps to a stated 0.1 lattice; keyboard movement adds 0.1, or 0.5 with Shift, to the unrounded displayed coordinate. Numeric Enter preserves fine precision. Values outside −5…5 are visibly rejected; pointer movement stops at that boundary.

Switch **Edit handles** to observations to move the data themselves. Editing data, centers or scales clears committed assignments and returns to a nearest-label preview. This prevents a historical assignment from masquerading as a new optimization run. Seed changes alone leave the current centers unchanged; **Initialize from seed** selects new initial observation rows.

Replay records deeply detached, frozen complete states. **First**, **Previous** and **Next** select them; they do not reverse mutations. Edit a center while viewing a historical frame and the new experiment begins with that frame's centers, observations and scales. Reset restores the literal original data and selected handle.

## Empty means, precision and evaluation

An empty cluster has no mean. The policy is **retain its previous center**. The membership table says “Undefined; retain” and shows the coordinates; it never reports the retained value as an observed empty-cluster mean. Such a center contributes zero to held WCSS. Part V deliberately does not reseed it behind the learner's back.

<Callout title="Finite input is only the first numerical condition" kind="numerical">
The pure model accepts 1–32 dense two-feature rows, unique nonempty IDs, 1 ≤ k ≤ n, positive finite scales and finite coordinates. It rejects overflowing differences, scaled differences, squares, sums and objectives. A nonzero difference whose scaled value or square becomes zero is rejected with rescaling guidance, so distinct points cannot silently become zero-distance ties. Ordinary rounding of small positive terms into a larger sum remains binary64 behavior.
</Callout>

WCSS has squared normalized-feature units. Smaller WCSS is not classification accuracy, truth, fairness, or usefulness. With distinct points, allowing $k=n$ can make WCSS zero by assigning each point its own center; that alone does not justify so many clusters. Choosing k requires the intended task, stability and an appropriate external or structural evaluation.

The physical plot uses equal raw x/y scales. Scaling changes the metric without redrawing a misleading transformed coordinate system. Numeric outputs are authoritative where observations coincide. Visible labels are sized for the measured viewport; wide tables and equations scroll in named bounded regions.
