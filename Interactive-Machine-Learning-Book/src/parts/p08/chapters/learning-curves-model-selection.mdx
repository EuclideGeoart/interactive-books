## A learning curve is a family of procedures

For nested training subsets T₁⊂T₂⊂⋯ and a fixed validation set V, let fⱼ be the model fitted on Tⱼ using a declared hyperparameter h. Two comparable descriptive errors are

$$
E_{\mathrm{train}}(j)=\frac1{|T_j|}\sum_{i\in T_j}(f_j(x_i)-y_i)^2,
\quad E_{\mathrm{val}}(j)=\frac1{|V|}\sum_{i\in V}(f_j(x_i)-y_i)^2.
$$

The horizontal coordinate is the actual row count |Tⱼ|, not a percentage without a denominator. Nested membership gives a controlled interpretation of “more data”: the larger dataset contains the smaller one. It does not guarantee that added groups are equally informative or representative. These prefixes follow seeded group order and preserve group boundaries.

Training error and validation error have the same units, squared target units. The ridge **objective** is different:

$$
J_j=E_{\mathrm{train}}(j)+\lambda w_j^2.
$$

The table shows both, but the solid curve plots training MSE. Comparing validation MSE to a penalized objective would confuse fit error with a complexity term. Because the objective normalizes squared error by n, a fixed λ has the same per-observation convention at each size; implementations using unnormalized SSE must adjust their penalty convention accordingly.

## Derive a two-row fit by hand

Consider a separate literal training pair x=(0,2), y=(0,2), no missing values, linear feature and λ=1. The fitted center is 1, population scale is 1 and transformed feature values are (−1,1). Feature and target means are 0 and 1. Feature variance and feature-target covariance are both 1. Therefore w=1/(1+1)=1/2 and b=1.

Predictions on the training pair are (1/2,3/2). Residuals prediction minus target are (1/2,−1/2), so training MSE is 1/4 and objective is 1/4+1·(1/2)²=1/2. For fixed validation x=4, y=4, the same fitted transform yields z=3, prediction 5/2 and validation MSE 9/4. The validation value does not refit the center or scale.

Adding another training pair would require a new fit. It is invalid to take the earlier w and move the plotted point to a larger sample count. The board computes every point from its actual subset and records fitted preprocessing and parameters in `data-numerical`.

## What curve shapes suggest

Large training and validation errors can suggest underfitting, but also a poor metric, corrupt labels, uninformative features or distribution shift. A large gap can suggest overfitting or validation mismatch. These are diagnostic hypotheses. Neither pattern uniquely identifies a causal explanation. More data can increase training error as a previously easy subset becomes more diverse, and validation error can rise when newly added groups pull the fit away from this particular validation group.

Fold-to-fold CV scores above the curve expose one source of sampling variability. This bounded lab uses one nested prefix sequence and one fixed validation fold; it does not invent confidence ribbons or claim that three points estimate a variance distribution. To estimate uncertainty, repeat the entire declared sampling procedure and summarize genuinely fitted repeats, respecting group or time boundaries. More curve points alone do not make them independent repeats.

## Canonical fits and explorer

X stores n×1 row observations after one scalar engineered feature; y is n×1, w is 1×1 and b is scalar. The closed-form fit recomputes all subset means. Constant features use a declared scale-one convention; with zero variance and λ=0, zero slope gives the mean-target prediction. All-missing training features are rejected because no observed training mean exists.

## Limits and connections

The one-feature quadratic option can describe a U-shaped response while the linear option cannot; this connects to <a href={href('/chapters/polynomial-features/')}>III-11</a>. It is still not an arbitrary polynomial expansion. Scaling changes the numerical meaning of a ridge penalty, so preprocessing and hyperparameters form one fitted procedure. Missingness itself may be informative; a production experiment would examine an indicator feature or domain-specific imputation rather than assuming the mean is adequate.

The displayed toy errors cannot guarantee deployment quality. Examine data provenance, group representativeness, costs of errors and future drift before deciding to collect more data or deploy a model.
