A group consists of two related observations; keeping both together models repeated measurements from one subject. Every development group appears in one validation fold and in the other training folds. The test group appears in neither role.

## Why a second dataset is needed

Training error describes how well a fitted model explains the observations it used. Validation asks how a procedure performs on observations it did not fit. Hyperparameters, including feature power and regularization strength, are choices about that procedure. Choosing them by validation makes validation data part of model development. A final test estimates performance of the selected procedure on a further held-out set, provided we do not keep changing the procedure after seeing that test.

This lab has 12 hand-authored observations in six groups. One whole group is test; five are development. With three folds, validation contains two, two and one development groups. Unequal fold sizes are visible. Group independence is a sampling assumption, not a consequence of these IDs. For time series, a random group split may still expose the future; a chronological boundary would be required instead.

## Define the fold score and grid

For fold k, fit every preprocessing statistic and the model on training set Tₖ, then evaluate on validation set Vₖ:

$$
E_k(h)=\frac1{|V_k|}\sum_{i\in V_k}(\hat y_i^{(T_k,h)}-y_i)^2,
\qquad \overline E(h)=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K E_k(h).
$$

The score weights folds equally, even when they have different row counts. A pooled per-row MSE would be another declared convention; it is not silently substituted here. Inspect every fold score before comparing their mean. Variability between folds can be more informative than an extra decimal in the mean.

The grid is ordered lexicographically: power p is 1 then 2; within each power, λ is 0, 1 then 4. Power chooses one feature φ=z or φ=z² after training-only imputation and scaling. This is one engineered scalar feature, not a degree-two model containing both z and z². The objective is

$$
J(w,b)=\frac1{|T_k|}\sum_{i\in T_k}(b+w\phi_i-y_i)^2+\lambda w^2.
$$

The intercept is not penalized. Eliminating b by setting its derivative to zero gives b=ȳ−wφ̄. Substituting into the w derivative gives

$$
w=\frac{\operatorname{mean}[(\phi-\bar\phi)(y-\bar y)]}
{\operatorname{mean}[(\phi-\bar\phi)^2]+\lambda}.
$$

At zero feature variance with λ=0, the slope is unidentified; this lab chooses w=0 and b=ȳ. Selection uses held-out MSE, not the training objective including λw². Changing λ would otherwise also change the quantity being compared.

## A literal selection example

Suppose two candidates have three held-out fold MSEs (1,4,1) and (2,2,2). Both have equal-weight CV mean 2. Our strict less-than update keeps the first candidate in grid order. There is no hidden tolerance that converts near ties into ties. If the first candidate has p=1, λ=0, these values stay fixed when refitting; the model's numerical w and b are fitted again on all development rows.

If validation fold sizes are (2,4,2), the first candidate's pooled row MSE is (2·1+4·4+2·1)/8=2.5, while its equal-fold score remains 2. Declaring aggregation is part of defining the experiment. Neither score should include the test rows.

## Explorer: which boundary changes the result?

Change the fold count between two and four and inspect the new memberships. Seed replay uses sorted group IDs followed by descending Fisher–Yates swaps driven by the frozen u32 LCG. Whole groups are distributed round-robin; this is **group-aware allocation, not stratification**. A classification experiment that claims stratification must allocate class counts deliberately. This regression board makes no such claim.

Use constant data to inspect the zero-variance convention and exact grid ties. Restore original data and inspect a missing feature: its imputed value is learned separately in each training fold. A global imputer would leak validation features into fitting. <a href={href('/chapters/preprocessing-feature-pipelines/')}>III-38</a> makes that numerical difference explicit.

<Callout title="Repeated selection spends validation information" kind="misconception">
Searching many grids and reporting only the best mean invites selection optimism. Nested CV can hold an outer validation layer around inner tuning. This small board demonstrates a single declared grid and a final group test; it does not implement nested CV or prove an unbiased population estimate.
</Callout>

## Limits and connections

Six synthetic groups cannot establish production generalization. A single test group can have unusually easy or difficult rows. Leakage-free code still needs representative sampling, a defensible group definition and a decision-relevant metric. Do not call a validation minimum proof that one architecture is universally best. The regression objective connects to <a href={href('/chapters/regularization/')}>III-12</a>; sampling variability is the reason to inspect multiple folds. Future preprocessing belongs inside each fit, not outside the boundary.
