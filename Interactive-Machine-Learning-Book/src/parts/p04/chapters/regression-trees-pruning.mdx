## Grow first, then price the leaves

The opening tree fits targets [0,0,2,2,0,2]. It has four terminal leaves and zero training MSE at depth 3. Set **Pruning strength α** to 0.25: two leaves remain, normalized training risk is 0.5 and cost is 1. At α=0.5, one leaf remains, predicts the mean 1 and has cost 1.5. The fewer-leaf tie policy matters at these breakpoints.

Change **Maximum depth** to 1, then restore 3. Pruning searches subtrees of the grown tree; it cannot create a branch that was never grown. Increase **Minimum leaf count** to constrain legal children before fitting. Replay pruning strengths to compare the same grown tree at α=0, 0.25, 0.5 and 1. Each selected frame supplies the predictions, node ledger, cost and accessible tree.

## Why a regression leaf predicts a mean

For a terminal region containing targets yi, choose a constant c. Its squared-error objective and derivative are

$$
\mathrm{SSE}(c)=\sum_{i\in S}(y_i-c)^2,\qquad
\frac{d}{dc}\mathrm{SSE}=2|S|c-2\sum_{i\in S}y_i.
$$

For nonempty S the unique minimizer is c=mean(y in S). Its SSE is the sum of squared centered targets; SSE/|S| is the node's mean squared error. This is a population-normalized empirical variance, not the unbiased sample variance with denominator |S|−1.

A tree maps each query to one leaf mean. X has n sample rows and two feature columns; y and predictions are conceptual n by 1 columns. It adds no intercept column. The row-index plot shows exact per-source targets and predictions, connected only by residual segments. It does not imply interpolation between adjacent rows or a smooth one-dimensional function.

## Search by squared-error reduction

The legal threshold policy is unchanged: equality goes left, duplicates stay together, and each child meets minimum leaf count. Node impurity is SSE/n, so the weighted-child expression simplifies:

$$
\Delta=\frac{\mathrm{SSE}_P-\mathrm{SSE}_L-\mathrm{SSE}_R}{n_P}.
$$

For the six-row root, mean is 1 and SSE is 6. At x1 ≤ 2.5, left mean is 0 with SSE 0; right targets [2,2,0,2] have mean 1.5 and SSE 3. Gain is (6−3)/6=0.5. The depth-1 tree has normalized risk 3/6=0.5. Further splits isolate the zero target at s5, giving zero training error with four leaves.

This greedily decreases training squared error. It can also fit noise, produce abrupt boundaries and extrapolate a constant terminal value far beyond observed features. A deeper tree's lower training objective is not evidence of lower validation error.

## Derive cost-complexity pruning

For a subtree T of the grown tree, let |T| denote its number of terminal leaves. We minimize

$$
C_\alpha(T)=R(T)+\alpha|T|,
\qquad R(T)=\frac1{n_{\mathrm{root}}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathrm{leaves}(T)}\mathrm{SSE}_\ell.
$$

The denominator is the original root count, including bootstrap multiplicity if present; each leaf is not separately reweighted to one vote. α has units of mean squared target error per leaf.

At each branch, compare collapsing it to its own mean with retaining its optimally pruned children. This recursive dynamic program finds the minimum cost among all subtrees of the already-grown tree. It does not solve the larger problem of growing every possible tree. Cost ties prefer fewer leaves.

## Check a nonbinary mean and a numerical tie

For targets [1,1,5,5,5] at x=0,1,2,3,4, root mean is 17/5 and SSE is 96/5. A two-leaf split at 1.5 has zero SSE. The costs meet when 96/25+α=2α, hence α=96/25=3.84.

## Assumptions, limits and connections

Constant targets give a zero-risk leaf; constant features with varying targets give the best mean without a legal split. Empty data have no mean or normalized cost. Nonfinite inputs, malformed shapes and overflowing residual squares are rejected rather than becoming silent NaNs. At very small target scales, squared deviations can underflow to zero; the stopping reason reports unresolved finer variation rather than claiming mathematically constant targets. Numeric inputs in the sheet are bounded to ±10; the pure kernels accept broader finite binary64 values subject to finite arithmetic.

Use validation data to choose depth, minimum leaf size and α, then evaluate the selected policy on separate test data. This lesson exposes training risk only and does not pretend its hand-authored rows provide a generalization estimate. III-23 uses many resampled trees, while III-25 fits regression trees to residual targets rather than directly to the original response.
