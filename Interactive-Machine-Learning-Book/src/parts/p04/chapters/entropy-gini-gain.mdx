## Impurity summarizes the label distribution

For binary labels in nonempty node S, p0=n0/n and p1=n1/n. Two common impurities are

$$
I_G(S)=1-p_0^2-p_1^2=2p_0p_1,\qquad
H(S)=-p_0\log_2p_0-p_1\log_2p_1.
$$

Gini is zero for pure labels and reaches 1/2 at equal counts. It is the probability of a mismatch between two independently drawn labels from this empirical distribution. Entropy is expected self-information in bits; it is zero when the label is certain and reaches 1 bit at equal binary proportions.

The limit of p log p as p approaches zero is zero. We implement that convention by skipping zero counts, not by evaluating log(0) and multiplying infinity by zero. Empty data do not define probabilities; our zero empty-node impurity is a bookkeeping convention, while the prediction remains undefined.

## Derive the weighted gain

Draw a row uniformly from the current node. It lands in the left child with probability nL/n and in the right with probability nR/n. Expected remaining impurity is therefore

$$
I_{\mathrm{child}}=\frac{n_L}{n}I_L+\frac{n_R}{n}I_R,\qquad
\Delta=I_P-I_{\mathrm{child}}.
$$

For entropy, this reduction is empirical information gain between the branch outcome and label. For Gini, it is a decrease in a different impurity. Both choose locally more homogeneous children; their numeric gains use different scales and must not be compared as though they were the same unit.

A useful Gini identity follows by letting a and b be child positive-label fractions and q=nL/n:

$$
\Delta_G=2q(1-q)(a-b)^2.
$$

It is nonnegative in exact arithmetic for a legal partition. The identity shows why a large difference between child fractions matters, but also why a tiny child receives a small weight. The runtime uses explicit child risks, while the oracle independently evaluates exact count fractions.

## Work the canonical root in bits

Counts [3,3] give parent H=1. At x1 ≤ 2.5, left counts [2,0] give H=0; right counts [1,3] give

$$
H_R=-\frac14\log_2\frac14-\frac34\log_2\frac34
\approx0.811278124459.
$$

The weighted child entropy is (2/6)0+(4/6)HR ≈ 0.540852082973. Gain is about 0.459147917027 bits.

At x1 ≤ 1.5, the left has one class-zero row and the right counts [2,3]. Right entropy is about 0.970950594455. Its weight is 5/6, yielding weighted entropy about 0.809125495379 and gain about 0.190874504621. Taking an unweighted average would assign the pure one-row child half the influence and overstate this split's benefit.

## Examine common confusions

Information gain is a training split score, not classification accuracy, a coefficient gradient, or a promise of causal information. At a leaf, this sheet predicts the majority label with class-zero ties. Changing the impurity can change the tree without changing that terminal decision policy.

A feature with many possible values offers more opportunities to find a favorable training split. Greedy gain alone does not correct selection bias or protect a held-out population. We support finite numeric features and two labels; missing values, categorical grouping and multiway branching require additional policies.

<Callout title="Exact identities, approximate logarithms" kind="numerical">
Counts, IDs and legal memberships are exact. Entropy is a binary64 logarithmic calculation. The independent oracle uses 80-digit Decimal logarithms, and its small-case parity bound is absolute plus relative 2×10^-12. That score tolerance does not change candidate legality or the runtime's exact computed-gain tie rule.
</Callout>
